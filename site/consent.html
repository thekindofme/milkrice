Recording consent

Last updated 16 November 2026

Milkrice listens to your meetings and writes them down. It does this quietly: no bot joins the call and nothing tells the other people it's running. That makes it your job to tell them.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If you're unsure what applies to you, check with your employer or a lawyer.

Why it matters

Laws about recording conversations differ by country, by state and by situation. Some places allow it when one person on the call agrees, which can be you. Others, including several US states such as California, require everyone to agree. Privacy laws such as the GDPR can also apply to transcripts, because they contain what people said. Many employers and clients have their own policies on top of the law.

A transcript is a record of the conversation even when no audio is kept, so treat transcription like recording.

A simple habit

  • Say it at the start: "I'm using Milkrice to take notes. It transcribes on my Mac and nothing is shared. Is everyone OK with that?"
  • If anyone says no, stop the recording in Milkrice, or just take notes by hand.
  • Mention it in the invite for recurring meetings, so new people know too.
  • Be careful with sensitive calls: health, legal, HR and anything covered by a confidentiality agreement.
  • If you share Milkrice's notes, remember they may contain other people's words and mistakes.

What Milkrice does to help

  • Recording audio is off by default. Transcripts are kept, audio isn't, unless you turn recordings on.
  • You can delete a meeting, with its transcript and any recording, at any time.
  • Calendar auto-start is optional. You can choose to be notified instead, and stop any recording with one click.
