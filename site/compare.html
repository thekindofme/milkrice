How Milkrice compares

Last checked 20 November 2026. Prices and features change; if something here is wrong, open an issue and we'll fix it.

Milkrice in one line: free, MIT-licensed meeting notes for Apple Silicon Macs, with an iPhone app in beta. Both sides of the call are transcribed on your Mac, and the notes are written by the Claude Code or Codex you already use, or by Ollama with nothing leaving your machine.

Where Milkrice stands out

  • Your existing AI writes the notes. Milkrice hands the transcript to the Claude Code or Codex you're already signed into, so there's no new subscription or API key. Choose Ollama and nothing leaves your Mac at all.
  • Your agents can use your meetings, for free. A read-only milkrice CLI and MCP server let Claude Code, Codex and others search your meetings. Every passage is labelled as your notes, the AI summary or the transcript, so an agent never mistakes a model's guess for what you wrote.
  • No biometric storage. Speakers are told apart on your Mac, and their voiceprints are never written to disk.
  • A phone app that keeps audio on the phone. The iPhone app transcribes on the device with Apple's speech models and writes notes with Apple Intelligence, again with no account. Cloud notetakers upload what your phone hears.
  • Light and native. Written in Rust, about 100 MB of memory when idle.

Where others are better

  • Other platforms. Milkrice runs on Apple Silicon Macs and, in beta, on iPhone. Most alternatives also run on Windows, and several on Linux or Android.
  • A finished phone app. The Milkrice iPhone app records conversations in the room and transcribes them on the phone, but it's still in beta. Granola, Otter, Fireflies, Fathom and Jamie have shipped phone apps for years.
  • Teams. Shared workspaces, link sharing, CRM sync and admin controls. Milkrice is for one person.
  • Zero setup. Granola needs a sign-in and nothing else. Milkrice needs three macOS permissions, a model download, and Claude Code, Codex or Ollama for written notes. Transcription works without any of those.
  • Accuracy on hard audio. Large cloud speech models handle heavy accents, crosstalk and noisy rooms better than a small local model.
  • Maturity. Meetily has tens of thousands of stars and Anarlog has a funded team. Milkrice is a young project from one developer.

Choosing

Choose Milkrice if you're on an Apple Silicon Mac (and maybe an iPhone), you want audio to stay on your machine, and you'd rather use the AI subscription you already have than add another one.

Choose something else if you need Windows, Linux or Android, team sharing or CRM sync, or a tool that works with no setup.

With any notetaker that doesn't join as a bot, telling people you're taking notes is your responsibility. See recording consent.
