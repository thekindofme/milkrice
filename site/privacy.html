Privacy

Last updated 22 November 2026

The short version. We don't collect anything about you. The Milkrice apps have no account, no telemetry, no analytics and no server of ours. Your notes, transcripts and audio stay on your Mac and iPhone. Text leaves your Mac only when Milkrice asks the AI provider you chose to write something, and that goes to the provider under your own account. This website counts visits and downloads anonymously, without cookies.

What we collect

Nothing. The people who make Milkrice do not receive your meetings, notes, transcripts, audio, calendar, contacts, usage data or crash reports. There is no sign-in and no licence check that phones home.

This website

This website sets no cookies and has no ads or third-party trackers. To see how people find Milkrice, we count visits and downloads in aggregate. None of this identifies you or follows you to other sites.

  • Visits. Cloudflare, which hosts this site, counts page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics: the page, the site that linked to it, browser and device type, and country. It uses no cookies or fingerprinting.
  • Download clicks. The Download buttons go through /download, which adds one to a count, noting the page or outside site you came from (its domain only), any campaign tag in the link, your country and platform (Mac, iPhone and so on), and then sends you to GitHub. Your IP address and browser details aren't stored.
  • GitHub and the App Store. GitHub shows us totals for visits to the repository and downloads of each release, including the Mac app's check for updates. Apple shows us App Store totals, and shares usage figures only from people who chose to share them with developers.

The fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address and browser details when it serves them. Our web host may keep standard server logs, such as IP address and pages requested, for a short time to operate the site.

What Milkrice stores on your Mac

Everything lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Milkrice:

  • milkrice.sqlite: your notes, AI-written summaries, transcripts, speaker names, chat history about meetings, and your settings (including any calendar feed addresses you add).
  • models/: the speech and speaker models you downloaded.
  • audio/: recordings, only if you turn recordings on (they're off by default). Each is a compressed .m4a with your microphone on one channel and the call audio on the other, about 60 MB an hour. Milkrice deletes recordings older than your retention setting (30 days by default; 0 keeps them until you delete them), and deleting a meeting deletes its recordings.

When recordings are off, audio is processed in memory and discarded once it has been transcribed. While a recording is being made, uncompressed audio is written to the same folder and converted when the meeting ends. Requests to an AI provider run in a temporary folder (milkrice-llm in the system temporary directory); the output file used for Codex is deleted after it's read.

To remove everything, quit Milkrice and delete the folder above.

Permissions Milkrice asks for

  • Microphone, to hear you.
  • Screen & System Audio Recording, to hear the other people on a call. Milkrice only captures audio; it never captures your screen.
  • Calendars, read-only, to know when meetings start and end and who was invited.
  • Notifications, to tell you when a meeting is starting.

When information leaves your Mac

The AI provider you choose

Milkrice writes notes, titles meetings, matches speakers to names and answers questions using an AI provider you pick. Unless you turn it off, this happens automatically after each meeting. For each request Milkrice sends the meeting's title, date, invited names, your notes and the transcript text (shortened in the middle for very long meetings). It never sends audio.

  • Claude Code: Milkrice runs the claude command you installed, using the Haiku model by default, with tools turned off and session saving disabled. The request goes to Anthropic under your Claude account.
  • Codex: Milkrice runs the codex command you installed, in a read-only sandbox with an ephemeral session. The request goes to OpenAI under your ChatGPT or OpenAI account.
  • Ollama: Milkrice talks to Ollama at 127.0.0.1:11434 on your own Mac. Nothing leaves your computer.

Anthropic's and OpenAI's privacy policies and terms, and the data settings of your plan, apply to what you send them. Milkrice never sees or stores your sign-in details or API keys; it uses the tools you have already signed into.

Model downloads

When you download a speech model, Milkrice fetches it from huggingface.co (or download.moonshine.ai for the Moonshine streaming model). Each file is checked against a pinned checksum before use. The speaker recognition model comes from the sherpa-onnx releases on GitHub. Like any download, those hosts see your IP address and which file you asked for. Milkrice sends nothing else.

Update checks

Shortly after launch, and when you choose "Check for updates" in the menu bar, Milkrice asks github.com for the latest release information. GitHub sees your IP address and the request; Milkrice sends no identifiers. Updates are only installed if you agree, and are verified against a signature before they run.

Calendar feeds you add

Milkrice normally reads the calendars already synced to macOS. If you also paste an iCal address (for example Google Calendar's secret address), Milkrice fetches that address over HTTPS from time to time. The address is stored only in your local settings.

Your agents

If you connect Milkrice to Claude Code or Codex from Settings, those agents can read your meetings through the milkrice command or MCP server, on your Mac and read-only. Anything an agent reads becomes part of that agent's session, which is handled by its provider under your account.

The iPhone app (beta)

The iPhone app works the same way: no account, no telemetry, nothing sent to us.

  • Storage. Meetings, notes and transcripts are kept inside the app on your iPhone, with iOS file protection. Recordings are deleted once they're transcribed unless you turn on "Keep audio". Deleting the app deletes everything.
  • Transcription uses Apple's on-device speech model. iOS may download that model from Apple the first time.
  • Notes are written on the iPhone with Apple Intelligence, where your iPhone supports it.
  • Permissions: the microphone, speech recognition, and the camera, which is used only to scan the pairing code your Mac shows.
  • Sync with your Mac is off until you pair. When it's on, meetings travel through a folder in your own iCloud Drive, end-to-end encrypted with a key that only your paired devices hold. Apple stores the encrypted files and can't read them. Audio is synced only if you turn that on. If you ask your Mac to write a note, the Mac sends it to the AI provider chosen there, as described above.

Children

Milkrice isn't directed at children and we don't knowingly collect anyone's information, children's included.
